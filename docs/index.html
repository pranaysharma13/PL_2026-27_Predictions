Finishing, over seven seasons

Goals scored minus goals expected

Expected goals counts the quality of chances a club creates. Score more than that and you are finishing well, or riding luck; the model treats it as a mix of both, which is why it fits on a blend of goals and expected goals rather than either alone.

Does it actually work

Every number above is worthless without this part. The model is tested by walking through past seasons week by week, fitting only on what was known at the time, predicting the next round, and scoring it against what happened.

Prediction error, lower is better

Fitting on expected goals beat fitting on goals in all three seasons. The bookmaker's closing price beat both, every time, by about the same margin. That is not a flaw so much as a fact about betting markets, and it is why this site has no odds on it.

How often reality landed inside the range

Standing at matchweek four across seven seasons, only 68.6% of clubs finished inside a band meant to hold 80%. The projections were confidently too narrow. Stretching the spread by 1.5 fixes the number.

We do not know why 1.5. Two explanations were tested and both failed. Letting club strength drift over the season needed a magnitude three times the entire gap between the best and worst clubs in the league, which is not a description of football. Loosening the model's shrinkage moved the number the wrong way. The remaining suspect is the resampling method used to estimate how uncertain the ratings are, and that is untested. So the correction stands, calibrated against seven seasons of history, with no mechanism behind it. Every projection here carries that caveat.
